About El Salvador

El Salvador is Central America’s smallest mainland country, but it brings together Pacific beaches, volcanic landscapes, archaeological sites, coffee highlands, colorful towns and busy cities within short geographic distances.

Essential facts

Language and money

Spanish is the official language. The U.S. dollar (USD) is the everyday reference currency. Bitcoin may be available in some places, but acceptance varies—ask first and keep cash or cards as a backup.

Climate and packing

The climate is generally tropical and warm, while volcanic and mountain areas can feel much cooler. Rain, heat and road conditions vary by season and elevation. Pack sun protection, light rain protection, water and appropriate footwear.

Electricity and connectivity

Electricity commonly uses 110–120 V, 60 Hz and North American-style plugs. Wi-Fi and mobile data are widely available in populated areas, but coverage can weaken in remote mountains, islands and rural roads.

Food and tipping

Pupusas are the best-known national dish, with corn or rice dough and many fillings. A service charge may already appear on restaurant bills; check before adding a tip. Tell staff about food allergies and ingredients you avoid.

Entry requirements

Passport validity, visa or tourist-card requirements, permitted stay, fees and vaccination rules depend on nationality, residence and travel history and can change. Verify them shortly before departure with El Salvador’s immigration authority, your airline and the nearest Salvadoran diplomatic mission. Do not rely only on an older travel article.

Getting around

Public buses are inexpensive but may require transfers and local boarding knowledge. Pre-booked airport transportation, private drivers and rental cars can simplify luggage, late arrivals and remote routes. Travel times can change with traffic, weather and road works.

What to experience

  • Pacific coast: surfing, beaches, seafood and sunsets.
  • Volcanic west: Santa Ana Volcano, Cerro Verde and Lake Coatepeque.
  • Coffee highlands: Ruta de las Flores, markets, murals and cooler weather.
  • Culture and archaeology: Joya de Cerén, Tazumal, San Andrés and historic centers.
  • Eastern El Salvador: El Cuco, Punta Mango, Morazán, Conchagua and the Gulf of Fonseca.

Practical habits

  • Carry small USD bills and do not assume every business accepts cards or Bitcoin.
  • Download maps and save reservations or important contacts offline.
  • Confirm opening hours, access rules and weather on the day of travel.
  • Use registered providers for specialized guiding or transportation and get prices in writing.
  • Keep passports and valuables secure and follow current local and official travel guidance.
