Towns and cities in El Salvador

El Salvador’s cities and villages reveal Indigenous heritage, colonial architecture, murals, markets, coffee culture and contemporary urban life. The official tourism collection includes 38 unique destinations; group nearby places into regional routes to reduce travel time.

Five excellent places to begin

San Salvador

Use the capital for museums, food, nightlife and the revitalized Historic Center, then connect to El Boquerón and nearby cities.

Show the Historic Center on our map →

Suchitoto

Cobblestone streets, colonial character, cultural spaces and views toward Lake Suchitlán make it one of the country’s most rewarding town visits.

Show on our map →

Concepción de Ataco and Ruta de las Flores

Ataco combines murals, coffee and cool weather. Pair it with Apaneca, Juayúa and Nahuizalco for a multi-town western route.

Show Ataco on our map →

La Palma and San Ignacio

Two cool-climate northern towns associated with crafts, mountain scenery and access toward Miramundo and El Pital.

Alegría and Berlín

Highland towns in Usulután with cooler air, coffee-country scenery and access to Alegría Lagoon.

Practical planning

  • Visit market towns early and confirm market days locally.
  • Combine nearby villages rather than crossing the country repeatedly.
  • Confirm the last bus before evening when returning from small towns.
  • Ask permission before photographing people, religious events or artisans.
