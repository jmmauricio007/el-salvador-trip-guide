El Salvador bus guide

Public buses are an inexpensive way to travel, but route numbers, departure points and schedules can change. Use this guide to understand the main connections, then confirm the route with your accommodation, terminal staff or driver on the day of travel.

Popular starting routes

Airport to San Salvador

Route 138 is commonly used from the public stop outside the airport complex. Travelers arriving late or carrying luggage may prefer a pre-booked verified shuttle.

San Salvador to La Libertad and the surf coast

Routes 102 and 102A connect the capital with La Libertad. Coastal services continue toward beaches such as El Tunco and El Zonte; confirm the final destination before boarding.

San Salvador to Santa Ana

Route 201 services depart from Terminal de Occidente. Direct and special buses may differ in stops, comfort and fare.

San Salvador to San Miguel

Route 301 serves the eastern part of the country. Confirm the current terminal and choose a direct service when available.

Before boarding

  • Carry small US-dollar bills and coins.
  • Travel during daylight and leave early for routes requiring transfers.
  • Show the destination name to the driver or fare collector.
  • Keep your passport, phone and money with you.
  • Confirm the final return departure before exploring rural areas.

All fares and travel times on the interactive guide are estimates, not guaranteed schedules.

Prefer a private tourist vehicle?

The official El Salvador tourism website maintains a directory of tourist-transport companies across the country. Confirm the provider’s current registration, insurance, vehicle, pickup point and complete price before booking.
