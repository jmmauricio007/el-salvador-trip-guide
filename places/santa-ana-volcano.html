Santa Ana Volcano (Ilamatepec)

Santa Ana Volcano, also called Ilamatepec, is El Salvador’s highest volcano. The summit experience is known for its turquoise crater lake and views across the western volcanic landscape.

Elevation2,381 metres
DifficultyModerate · approximately 5/10
Suggested timeMost of one day
ConditionsCool, windy and changeable
View on interactive mapGet directions

What the hike is like

The ascent is generally considered moderate, but sun, wind, elevation, uneven ground and changing weather make preparation important. Access procedures and departure groups can change, so arrive early and confirm the current starting point and guide requirements.

What to bring

Wear closed hiking shoes and comfortable layers. Bring sufficient water, sun protection, sunglasses and a light rain layer. The crater contains acidic volcanic water; remain within authorized viewing areas and follow guide instructions.

Admission and hours

The official tourism page lists the volcano complex as open from 8:00 a.m. to 4:00 p.m. and lists foreign admission at US$3. Guide, parking or other charges may be separate. Verify all prices and the latest entry cutoff before departure.

Getting there

Most visitors arrive through the Cerro Verde or Los Volcanes area by private vehicle, organized excursion or a combination of bus and local transport. A tour simplifies the early departure and current access requirements.

Practical planning

  • Check official weather and volcanic-access information before leaving.
  • Do not schedule the hike immediately after a late-night arrival.
  • Carry cash for admission, guides, parking or local services.
  • Stay on the authorized trail and do not approach fumaroles.

Nearby experiences

Cerro Verde, Izalco Volcano, Lake Coatepeque, Santa Ana city and the Tazumal archaeological site.
