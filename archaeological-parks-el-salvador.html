Archaeological parks in El Salvador

Five official parks reveal different chapters of El Salvador's pre-Hispanic history—from a village preserved by volcanic ash to ceremonial centers, monumental pyramids and indigo traditions. All five are now connected to our interactive map.

The five official archaeological parks

Joya de Cerén · La Libertad

This UNESCO World Heritage Site preserves evidence of everyday Maya village life beneath volcanic ash. It offers a rare look at homes, crops and community spaces rather than only monumental buildings.

Show on our map →

San Andrés · La Libertad

A major regional center in the Zapotitán Valley, with an acropolis, plazas, pyramidal structures and a site museum. Its open green setting pairs well with Joya de Cerén.

Show on our map →

Cihuatán · San Salvador

A large fortified pre-Hispanic city north of the capital, with pyramids, plazas, a ball court, an archaeological trail and a museum. The exposed site can be hot.

Show on our map →

Tazumal · Santa Ana

Chalchuapa's best-known archaeological complex features an imposing stepped structure and a site museum. Combine it with Casa Blanca on the same outing.

Show on our map →

Casa Blanca · Santa Ana

A ceremonial center in Chalchuapa with earthen structures, a museum containing a Maya-inscribed stela fragment, an archaeological window and an indigo workshop.

Practical planning

  • Confirm opening days, admission fees and guided-tour availability before leaving.
  • Bring water, sun protection, insect repellent and comfortable shoes; several sites have limited shade.
  • Do not climb structures unless signs or park staff explicitly allow it.
  • Allow time for the museums—the artifacts explain what is difficult to see from the structures alone.
