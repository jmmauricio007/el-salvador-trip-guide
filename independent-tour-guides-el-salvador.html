Match the guide to your trip

Language and communication

Ask which languages the guide uses professionally—not only conversationally. For a history, archaeology or nature tour, clear explanations and the ability to answer questions matter.

Region and local knowledge

A guide familiar with western volcanoes may not specialize in Morazán, the Gulf of Fonseca or eastern surf areas. Share your route and ask about recent local access, opening hours and travel times.

Surf, food and community experiences

A cultural guide is not automatically a surf instructor, boat captain or food-safety expert. Confirm the role of every person involved and which activities are operated by third parties.

Questions before booking

  • What is your full name and current tourism-guide credential or registration?
  • Which languages, regions and subjects do you guide professionally?
  • What is included: entrances, transportation, meals, equipment and taxes?
  • Will you personally lead the tour, and who supplies any vehicle, boat or specialist activity?
  • What are the meeting point, duration, group size and accessibility requirements?
  • What happens if weather, park access, illness or a flight delay changes the plan?
  • What payment method, receipt, cancellation and refund terms apply?

Guide versus driver

A licensed or experienced guide explains destinations and manages the visitor experience; a driver provides transportation. One person may offer both, but never assume it. Confirm driving authorization, passenger insurance and the vehicle separately.
